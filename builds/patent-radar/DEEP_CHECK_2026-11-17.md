# RANKED SHORTLIST — Top 8 across all 33

Ranked by legal clarity × market evidence × ease of making. Boring-but-clear-and-sellable beats glamorous-but-risky throughout.

## 1. US4076244 — Foosball playing piece (1978)

## 2. USD165508 — Budlong radio cabinet (1951) → Bluetooth re-house
**CLEAR-ISH · RE-HOUSE · Legal H / Market H / Ease M.** No assignee, no brand, no trade dress. Printed/wood cabinet + Bluetooth amp + modern speaker; the largest print of the top 8, but the money is demonstrated.

## 3. USD174098 — Hand telephone (1955) → Bluetooth handset
**CLEAR-ISH · RE-HOUSE · Legal H / Market H / Ease M.** North Electric is a dead brand and the curved handset is the most generic phone form in existence. Bluetooth-converted vintage phones sell at $195–$285 on Etsy; a printed shell + Bluetooth guts + ballast (so it feels like bakelite) credibly fills the $60–$120 gap. Stay with the conventional handset form — don't drift toward the Ericofon "cobra" silhouette.

## 4. USD120511 — Lux wall clock (1940) → quartz re-house
**CLEAR-ISH · RE-HOUSE · Legal H / Market M / Ease H.** Lux Clock Co. was absorbed in 1961 — no living clock brand polices this shape. Printed case + commodity quartz movement + printed dial is the easiest *complete, working product* in the set, in a proven collector category ($20–$75 originals; replica band est. $35–$75). Keep the Lux name and dial lettering off.

## 5. US6415689 — Corkscrew for a wine steward (2001)
**CLEAR-ISH · METAL FAB / RE-HOUSE · Legal H / Market H / Ease L.** Term-expired 2021, whole family dead. Its companion worm patent US9604832 (CAUTION, BE/ES family still listed active) is an optional component only — a straight-worm build doesn't need it.

## 6. USD201144 — Derby alarm clock (1965) → quartz
**CLEAR-ISH · PRINT+HARDWARE · Legal H / Market M / Ease H.** Dead Swiss brand, generic mid-century case form, and a genuinely buildable working product (printed case + a few dollars of quartz alarm movement). ## 7. USD289896 — Technophone pocket telephone (1987) → prop
**CLEAR-ISH · PRINT (prop) · Legal H / Market M / Ease H.** Best story-to-effort ratio in the set: the historically real "first pocket phone" (originals valued £600–£1,500 — collector value a replica must never claim), sold honestly as an unbranded $25–$40 1980s brick-phone prop, a format Etsy already moves at $14–$40. Boxy full-scale print, minimal supports, near-zero legal exposure (Technophone is dead; the shape was never Nokia's in buyers' minds).

## 8. USD75188 — Rice table lighter (1928)
**CLEAR-ISH · PRINT+HARDWARE · Legal H / Market M / Ease M.** The cleanest lighter of the whole 33: expired ~1942, Bernard Rice's Sons/"Apollo" survives only as an antique mark, and 1920s table lighters are a generic collector category (era comps sell $100–$150 at auction). Printed body + modern refillable butane insert — never replicate a petrol/wick mechanism, never use the Rice/Apollo name.

## 2. US4080530 — Equapoised Lamp / Counterbalanced Arm Assemblies

- **ODP record:** App. 05735988; granted 1978-03-21; term estimate expired 1995-03-21.
- **Status verdict: CAUTION.** The patent itself is clean: Google Patents **Expired – Lifetime**, filed 1976-10-27 (Jens Christian Krogsrud), Norwegian priorities NO753610/11 (1975). Its one US child, **US4160536A "Counterbalanced arm"** (filed 1978-03-20, Jac Jacobsen AS), is also **Expired – Lifetime** (anticipated 1996-10-27) — family risk confirmed nil.
  - **What this patent actually is:** NOT the Anglepoise patent. This is the **Krogsrud / Jac Jacobsen (Luxo)** line: parallelogram arm pairs with the coil springs **fully enclosed inside the tubular arms**, springs loaded longitudinally — an improvement over the exposed-spring designs. The original Anglepoise mechanism is George Carwardine's 1932 patent (Herbert Terry), long expired; Jacobsen's Luxo L-1 (1937) itself grew out of English spring-balanced luminaires. So the *mechanism space* is public domain from several directions.
  - **The real risk is brand/trade dress, and it is live on two sides:** (1) **ANGLEPOISE** is a registered US trademark (USPTO serial 85754356, Anglepoise Holdings Ltd, covering electric lamps and adjustable stands) and the Original 1227 silhouette is the brand's identity — do not name, market, or style a copy as "Anglepoise" or clone the 1227's distinctive shade/base get-up. (2) **Luxo** is also a living brand (Glamox/Luxo) still selling the L-1, and the L-1 form carries the Pixar "Luxo Jr." association — do not trade on "Luxo" either. A generic enclosed-spring parallelogram arm sold under Airam's own name/design language is defensible; a lookalike sold as an "Anglepoise-style/Luxo-style lamp" is the exposure.
  - Also note: a lamp is a mains-electrical product — UL/ETL listing expectations for retail sale are a practical (non-patent) barrier the other items don't have.
- **Scores:** Legal clarity **M** (patent H, brand space L — blended M) · Market evidence **H** · Ease **L**
- **Market signal:** Strong but two-tiered. Premium originals still in production: Anglepoise Original 1227 desk lamp $220–$320 US retail (City Lights SF $220–240 variants; Lumens task lamp $288; maker's US list $320 per Nov-2026 comparison), floor $420; Luxo L-1 €187 at Finnish Design Shop (~C$294 listed). Floor: IKEA TERTIAL exposed-spring work lamp **$19.99** — a commodity generic already owns the bottom. The gap a build must hit is "better than TERTIAL, well under $220," against two heritage brands.
- **Make route: METAL FAB** (+ bought-in electricals) — steel tube arms, springs, pivot brackets, shade spinning/fab; printed parts only for joints/knobs prototyping. High effort: spring-rate tuning to actually equipoise is the hard part, plus electrical safety.
- **Bottom line:** Patent is dead and the mechanism is free, but you would be selling into a space owned in customers' minds by two live brands with a $20 IKEA floor — build only as an original-design, own-brand lamp, never as a replica.
